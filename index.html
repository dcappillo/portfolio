About

I make complicated things make sense.

My background is an unlikely combination of mathematics, federal program evaluation, and over a decade in classrooms where I had to make complex things make sense to people who were not convinced they needed to understand them. That last skill turns out to be useful everywhere.

At NASA Glenn Research Center I ran mixed-methods evaluations of national STEM programs. I designed research instruments, conducted focus groups and interviews, coded qualitative data in NVivo, and synthesized findings into reports that went to senior government leadership. I wanted to understand what people actually experienced, where the friction was, and what needed to change.

I hold a B.A. in Pure Mathematics and an M.Ed. in Evaluation and Measurement from Kent State University. I code in Python and have fun in HTML. I build things. I fix things. I figure things out. I am based in Fallston, Maryland and eligible to obtain a Public Trust or Secret clearance.

Independent Build · 2026

CapFootball: A Football GM Sim, Refactored Out of a Single File

Started as a single ~19,000-line HTML file, because that's how these things always start. Rebuilt it into 70 numbered source files stitched together by a custom Node.js build script, deliberately skipping ES modules to preserve the global scope the game's onclick handlers depend on. What runs on top: a full 32-team GM simulation with weekly game sim, roster and depth chart management, free agency, a live draft, trade logic, coaching staff, injuries, and season-long stat tracking. Built and debugged solo, including chasing down a null-coach crash affectionately known internally as the Black Monday bug.
